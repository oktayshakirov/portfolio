How to use it

  1. Press Copy prompt.
  2. Paste it into Claude, ChatGPT or any AI chat.
  3. Fill in the four lines under MY BOOK and send.
  4. Answer after each step. It waits for you before it moves on.

Start with what you really know: your work, a blog you have written for years, a problem you solved, something you have studied for a long time. The prompt is built to stop you from making a book out of someone else's.

Why I made it

You have probably seen the videos about making money with AI audiobooks: take a popular book, let AI rewrite it, add an AI voice and upload it. That is not your book. It is a copy of someone else's work.

I wanted to find out if there is an honest way to do it. So I made five audiobooks with AI in less than two months, almost five hours of listening, and none of them is a copy. Two of them sum up years of articles from my tinnitus and crypto blogs. One came from my own job search, and one from every mistake I wrote down while making videos with AI. This prompt is the process I follow for every book, in six steps.

The 6 steps it walks you through

1. The topic

Pick something you care about and something you already know. Not the bestseller everyone copies, and not a topic nobody would listen to. The prompt suggests topics built only from your own knowledge, says who each one helps, and turns down the ones that are really a summary of someone else's book.

2. The book

Copy the shape of what sells, never the content. A short audiobook is about an hour, a dozen or so chapters, one idea each. The prompt plans the chapters with a word budget for each, offers title options, and drafts a few chapters at a time, because a wrong tone is a quick fix in chapter three and a rewrite in chapter thirteen.

3. The facts

This one surprised me the most. On my first book I checked thirteen claims after the AI wrote them, and seven were wrong or bigger than the truth. Every mistake went the same way: more dramatic than the real story. The prompt puts every fact into a sources list before anything is recorded, and flags the drafts that sound more certain than the evidence.

4. The voice

Read it yourself or use an AI voice. If you use an AI voice, do not slow it down to sound calmer: a slowed synthetic voice usually sounds more like a robot, not less. Check how it says the names and the words in your title before you render the whole book. And tell every store it is an AI voice, because they require it.

5. The cover

An image model can draw the cover, but it misspells words. The prompt writes a cover prompt with one strong picture and a title that reads at thumbnail size, and reminds you to check every letter. My first book was rejected because the subtitle on the cover did not match the one in the form.

6. The stores

Every store has its own rules for AI narration and asks for different files: chapter tracks, samples, credits, a cover and sometimes a PDF. The prompt lists what to prepare, the rejections to avoid, and gives you a checklist to run before you upload.

What you get back

Topic ideas built from what you know, a chapter plan with word budgets and title options, a sources list for every claim, a pronunciation list for the voice, a cover prompt, and a pre-submit checklist for the stores you choose. It ends with an honest note on what the book can and cannot do. It never promises income.

Questions

Can you make money with AI audiobooks?

Maybe. But not by copying someone else's book. A short, genuinely helpful book on a topic you know is something people will listen to, and that is the only kind worth publishing.

Does it work with ChatGPT and Gemini, or only Claude?

It works in any AI chat. I use it with Claude, and nothing in it depends on one model.

Do I have to use an AI voice?

No. The prompt asks first, and the steps work for a book you read yourself.

Which stores can I publish to?

Many stores take audiobooks, directly or through a distributor, and their rules for AI narration differ and change. The prompt makes you check each store's current rules before you record anything.
