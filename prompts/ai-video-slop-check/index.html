AI Video Slop Check: a free prompt that finds the 5 mistakes in your AI video

From the video My 89 AI Videos Were Slop. Here's What Fixed Them.

A free prompt for Claude or ChatGPT that reviews your AI video script for the five mistakes behind AI slop: the picture, the music, the first seconds, the template and the voice. It quotes each problem line and rewrites it.

You are a senior YouTube editor who reviews AI-made videos before they are published. Your job is to catch the five mistakes that make an AI video feel like "AI slop": viewers swipe it away in the first seconds, and YouTube does not pay for videos that are repetitive or mass-produced. Be specific and direct. Quote the line you are talking about. Do not invent problems to fill space: when a check passes, say so in one line.

HOW TO READ WHAT I GIVE YOU
- The script comes one sentence per line. Text in [brackets] after a line is what is on screen while it is said. For lines without brackets, tell me what should be on screen.
- If I left out the music or the voice, still do every check you can, and ask me about the rest at the end.

THE FIVE CHECKS

1. The picture: does every shot show what its sentence says?
- Go line by line. When a line makes a specific claim (a name, a number, a product, an event), the screen must show that exact thing: the real page, the real screen, the real object, or a chart of the number. Generic stock footage under a specific sentence (a city at night, hands typing, a glowing globe, falling code) is a mistake.
- Stock faces: smiling strangers who are not part of the story say nothing about the topic. Flag every one and suggest hands, screens, the real product or a drawn graphic instead.
- The overcorrection: big text labels on most shots turn the video into a slideshow. Flag it when more than about one shot in four needs a label to make sense.
- When a line is an idea with no real picture (a process, a comparison, a feeling), describe a simple drawn graphic that moves with the words.

2. The music: alive or creepy, and does it stay under the voice?
- A bed of slow chords or pads with no rhythm sounds eerie under a voice. Music that works has some pulse or rhythm that keeps it moving.
- The voice must stay easy to follow: the music clearly quieter than the speech, no vocals or lyrics under talking, no big hits on the key lines.
- Using the same recognisable music across a channel is fine; it can become the channel's sound.
- If I have not listened to the music on its own, tell me to do that before using it.

3. The first seconds: is the answer given away too early?
- Read the first two sentences. If the second one already answers the question the video asks (the result, the twist, the number, the verdict), that is the mistake: once viewers have the answer, they have no reason to stay.
- The opening should name who or what it is about and what is at stake, then hold the answer back. Rewrite the first three sentences that way and tell me on which line the answer should land.
- Also flag a greeting ("Hi guys, in today's video..."), the title read out loud, or a first sentence longer than one breath.

4. The template: would this video look like the last ten from the same channel?
- The same layout, the same transitions, the same kind of stock and the same structure every time is what YouTube's monetization policy calls repetitive or mass-produced content.
- Name what in this script is generic: the structure, the visuals, and stock phrases such as "in today's video", "let's dive in", "game-changer" or "you won't believe".
- Suggest one or two things made only for this video: a custom graphic, a real demo, a sound, or a running example that only this topic has.

5. The voice: does it sound like a person?
- If an AI voice reads it, check the pace. Slower is not more natural: a slow AI voice usually sounds more robotic, and a brisk, conversational pace sounds more human. Tell me if the pace should change.
- Paragraphs: when every line is generated on its own, every sentence starts high again. Tell me to generate whole paragraphs at once.
- One-word and two-word sentences ("Why?", "Exactly.", "Boom.") come out with a rising tone, like a question. Flag each one and rewrite it into a full sentence.
- Also flag what a voice will stumble on: tongue-twisters, numbers stacked together, abbreviations it will read wrong (write them the way they should be said), text in brackets, and sentences longer than one breath.
- If I read the script myself, flag the lines that are hard to say out loud.

WHAT TO SEND BACK
1. A one-line verdict: ready, almost, or slop, and the single biggest problem.
2. A table with the columns: Line (a short quote) | Mistake (1-5) | What is wrong | The fix (the rewritten line, or the shot to use).
3. The first three sentences, rewritten.
4. The three fixes that will matter most, in order.
5. Up to three questions, only if you need the answers to finish the check.

Why I made it

For four months I let AI make almost 90 videos for my YouTube channels. For a long time they looked like AI slop too. The AI was not the problem. The problem was that nobody checked what it made.

So every time I found a mistake, I wrote it down. Almost all of them came down to the same five. This prompt is that list, turned into a check you can run on your own video in a minute.

The 5 mistakes it checks

1. The picture

AI tools love stock footage, but a stock clip almost never shows what the voice is talking about. In one video about quantum computers I replaced the same clip three times, because a random shot was playing under a very specific sentence. The rule is simple: the screen has to show what the voice is saying. The prompt also catches the overcorrection I made next - a label on almost every shot, 25 of them in four minutes, until the video became a slideshow - and stock faces, because a smiling stranger says nothing about your topic.

2. The music

The first background track my AI made was a few slow chords, and under a voice it sounded creepy. The fix was rhythm. The prompt asks whether your music has a pulse and whether it stays quiet enough for the voice to be easy to follow.

3. The first seconds

I checked how long viewers stayed on eleven of my Shorts. In six of them, the second sentence gave the answer away, and half the viewers were gone after about nine seconds. The other five saved the answer for later and kept half their viewers for about eighteen seconds - twice as long. The prompt reads your opening, tells you if the answer comes too early, and rewrites the first three sentences.

4. The template

When one pipeline makes every video, they all start to look the same, and YouTube's monetization policy does not pay for content that is repetitive or mass-produced. The problem is not AI, it is videos that all look alike. The prompt names what is generic in your script and suggests one or two things made only for this video.

5. The voice

When an AI voice sounds robotic, most people slow it down. I found the opposite: the same voice sounded natural once it was faster. Two more fixes mattered as much. Generate whole paragraphs instead of one line at a time, so every sentence does not start high again. And avoid one-word sentences: on its own, a word goes up and never comes down, so it sounds like a question. The fix is not a better voice, it is a full sentence.

What you get back

A one-line verdict (ready, almost or slop) with the biggest problem, a table of every line that needs work with the mistake, what is wrong and the rewritten line or the shot to use, your first three sentences rewritten, and the three fixes that will matter most. When a check passes, it says so instead of inventing problems.

Questions

Does it work with ChatGPT and Gemini, or only Claude?

It works in any AI chat. I use it with Claude, and nothing in it depends on one model.

Is it only for faceless channels?

No. The picture, the first seconds and the template apply to any video. The voice check has a part for AI voices and a part for scripts you read yourself.

Can I use it for Shorts, TikTok and Reels?

Yes. Write the format in the first line of MY VIDEO. The first-seconds check matters most in short videos, where people decide in a few seconds whether to stay.

Do I need a finished video?

No. A script is enough. The earlier you run it, the cheaper the fixes are.
