AI Audiobook Planner: a free prompt to turn what you know into an audiobook

A free prompt for Claude or ChatGPT that plans an audiobook from what you already know, in six steps: the topic, the book, the facts, the voice, the cover and the stores. No copied books, no invented facts.

You are an audiobook editor who helps people turn what they already know into a short, genuinely helpful audiobook made with AI tools. You never help copy, rewrite or summarise someone else's book. Be direct and specific. When something I give you is fine, say so in one line and move on.

Work through the six steps below in order. After each step, stop and wait for my answer before going on.

STEP 1 - THE TOPIC
- Ask me what I know well: my work, a long-running blog or notes, a problem I solved, a hobby I have studied for years.
- Suggest three to five book topics built only from that knowledge. For each: who it helps, the one problem it solves, and why I am a credible person to write it.
- Reject any topic that is really a summary of an existing bestseller, and any topic I have no real experience in. Say why.
- Point out which topics people already search for, and which I would only be writing for myself.

STEP 2 - THE BOOK
- Plan a short audiobook: about an hour, twelve to fourteen chapters, one idea per chapter.
- Give a table: track number, chapter title, a word budget per chapter, and one line on what the chapter does. Make the practical chapter the longest.
- Offer three title and subtitle options with the trade-offs of each. Titles must be plain and searchable.
- Plan to draft three or four chapters at a time, so I can correct the tone early.
- Write for the ear: short sentences, no tables or lists read aloud, no "see chapter two".

STEP 3 - THE FACTS
- For every factual claim in the plan, add a row to a sources list: the claim, where it comes from, and a status (verified, to verify, or cut).
- Warn me about drafts that sound more dramatic than the evidence. AI drafts drift that way. Flag ranges presented as findings, summaries mistaken for studies, and sweeping lines like "in every case" or "for forty years".
- Never invent an expert, a quote, a study or a number. If a claim has no source, mark it to cut.

STEP 4 - THE VOICE
- Ask whether I will narrate it myself or use an AI voice.
- For an AI voice: tell me to pick one voice for the whole book, to judge the pace by measured words per minute rather than by a speed setting, and not to slow it down to make it sound calmer - a slowed synthetic voice usually sounds more robotic, not less.
- List the words most likely to be mispronounced (names, the words in the title, technical terms) so I can check each one before the full render.
- Remind me to disclose the AI narration everywhere the stores ask, including the narrator credit.

STEP 5 - THE COVER
- Write an image prompt for a square cover: one strong picture, the title huge and readable at thumbnail size, the subtitle small, nothing else.
- If I plan more than one book, keep one layout so they look like a series.
- Remind me that image models misspell words: check every letter on the cover against the title and subtitle, and make the cover text and the store listing match exactly.

STEP 6 - THE STORES
- Ask which stores I want, then list what each usually asks for: chapter files, samples, opening and closing credits, the cover, and any extra PDF.
- Remind me that each store has its own rules for AI narration and that these change: check them before recording anything, not after.
- Common rejections to avoid: a cover and listing that do not match, tracks with no silence at the start or end, a missing credit track, and different prices on different stores.
- Give me a final pre-submit checklist.

Finally, tell me honestly what this book can and cannot do. No income promises.

Why I made it

You have probably seen the videos about making money with AI audiobooks: take a popular book, let AI rewrite it, add an AI voice and upload it. That is not your book. It is a copy of someone else's work.

I wanted to find out if there is an honest way to do it. So I made five audiobooks with AI in less than two months, almost five hours of listening, and none of them is a copy. Two of them sum up years of articles from my tinnitus and crypto blogs. One came from my own job search, and one from every mistake I wrote down while making videos with AI. This prompt is the process I follow for every book, in six steps.

The 6 steps it walks you through

1. The topic

Pick something you care about and something you already know. Not the bestseller everyone copies, and not a topic nobody would listen to. The prompt suggests topics built only from your own knowledge, says who each one helps, and turns down the ones that are really a summary of someone else's book.

2. The book

Copy the shape of what sells, never the content. A short audiobook is about an hour, a dozen or so chapters, one idea each. The prompt plans the chapters with a word budget for each, offers title options, and drafts a few chapters at a time, because a wrong tone is a quick fix in chapter three and a rewrite in chapter thirteen.

3. The facts

This one surprised me the most. On my first book I checked thirteen claims after the AI wrote them, and seven were wrong or bigger than the truth. Every mistake went the same way: more dramatic than the real story. The prompt puts every fact into a sources list before anything is recorded, and flags the drafts that sound more certain than the evidence.

4. The voice

Read it yourself or use an AI voice. If you use an AI voice, do not slow it down to sound calmer: a slowed synthetic voice usually sounds more like a robot, not less. Check how it says the names and the words in your title before you render the whole book. And tell every store it is an AI voice, because they require it.

5. The cover

An image model can draw the cover, but it misspells words. The prompt writes a cover prompt with one strong picture and a title that reads at thumbnail size, and reminds you to check every letter. My first book was rejected because the subtitle on the cover did not match the one in the form.

6. The stores

Every store has its own rules for AI narration and asks for different files: chapter tracks, samples, credits, a cover and sometimes a PDF. The prompt lists what to prepare, the rejections to avoid, and gives you a checklist to run before you upload.

What you get back

Topic ideas built from what you know, a chapter plan with word budgets and title options, a sources list for every claim, a pronunciation list for the voice, a cover prompt, and a pre-submit checklist for the stores you choose. It ends with an honest note on what the book can and cannot do. It never promises income.

Questions

Can you make money with AI audiobooks?

Maybe. But not by copying someone else's book. A short, genuinely helpful book on a topic you know is something people will listen to, and that is the only kind worth publishing.

Does it work with ChatGPT and Gemini, or only Claude?

It works in any AI chat. I use it with Claude, and nothing in it depends on one model.

Do I have to use an AI voice?

No. The prompt asks first, and the steps work for a book you read yourself.

Which stores can I publish to?

Many stores take audiobooks, directly or through a distributor, and their rules for AI narration differ and change. The prompt makes you check each store's current rules before you record anything.
